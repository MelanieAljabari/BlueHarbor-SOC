CASE-001: Investigation of Local TCP Port 33245
Date: August 14, 2026
Analyst: Melanie Aljabari

SUMMARY
During baseline network inspection of the Linux SOC lab, a TCP service was identified listening on port 33245. The service was investigated to determine the owning process, network exposure, and whether the activity appeared expected or suspicious.

INITIAL FINDING
A default Nmap scan of localhost found no open ports among the 1,000 commonly scanned TCP ports.

Command:
nmap localhost

A local socket inspection later identified TCP port 33245 listening on the loopback address.

Command:
ss -tuln

Finding:
127.0.0.1:33245 - LISTEN

INVESTIGATION

1. Process Identification
The listening socket was investigated with process information enabled.

Command:
sudo ss -tulnp

Result:
Port 33245 was owned by the containerd process.

2. Process Verification
The process was reviewed using its PID.

Command:
ps -fp 140

Result:
The process was confirmed as /usr/bin/containerd and was running as root.

3. Full TCP Port Scan
A scan of all TCP ports was performed.

Command:
nmap -p- localhost

Result:
33245/tcp open
All other scanned TCP ports were closed.

4. Service Detection
Nmap service/version detection was performed against port 33245.

Command:
nmap -sV -p 33245 localhost

Result:
The port was confirmed open, but Nmap could not identify the service by name. The service returned HTTP-style responses.

5. HTTP Test
The service was queried directly with curl.

Command:
curl -i --max-time 5 http://127.0.0.1:33245/

Result:
HTTP/1.1 404 Not Found
Content-Type: text/plain

The response confirmed that an HTTP-capable service was responding on the port.

6. Service Status
The containerd service was reviewed using systemctl.

Command:
sudo systemctl status containerd --no-pager

Result:
containerd.service was loaded, enabled, and active (running).
Main PID: 140
No service failures or suspicious error messages were observed.

ANALYSIS
Port 33245 was bound to 127.0.0.1, meaning the service was accessible only from the local Linux environment and was not listening on all network interfaces. Multiple tools independently confirmed that containerd owned the listening socket.

CONCLUSION
The activity appears consistent with an expected containerd service in the Linux environment. No evidence of malicious or unauthorized activity was identified during this investigation.

DISPOSITION
Closed - Expected system activity.
